## A little about me

I study information science and engineering, and I like bringing what I learn into everyday life. Sometimes that starts with a task I keep repeating; sometimes it's just an idea I want to try. I enjoy poking around, changing a few things, and seeing what happens. It doesn't always have to be useful.

My background includes robotics, control, gesture-based interaction, and data analysis. Alongside university, I've worked in Japanese rental operations. Moving between study and practical work has made me interested in how people use information, make decisions, and keep complicated tasks moving.

I'm especially curious about the space between using a tool and making one: understanding enough to adjust it, connect it to something else, or turn a small idea into an experiment. That's also how I tend to approach learning—start with something I care about, try it, and work through the parts I don't understand yet.

## What I'm working on

**Customer lifecycle management for Japanese rental services**

I'm working on an AI-assisted system informed by my rental-work experience. The focus is the customer journey from the first inquiry through application, screening, contracts, and move-in follow-up: keeping track of the current stage, what has changed, and what needs to happen next. I'm exploring how to express that workflow as states, transitions, and tasks, with AI supporting information handling and human decisions.

**Master's research on electronic petitions**

At the Digital Governance Systems Laboratory, I'm studying petition outcomes on Taiwan's JOIN platform. I'm interested in how petition text, the way public support develops over time, and wider social attention relate to whether a petition succeeds. The research is ongoing, with the aim of understanding these relationships and exploring how they could inform support for petitioners.
